## 1. Objective and estimand

### 1.1 The question

Every headline this project has produced scores a campaign by the terminal **simple
regret** of one nominated recipe. K6 and K6b ask whether a *different* deliverable — a
certified operating region, which is what a manufacturing batch record actually contains —
ranks the same eight campaign designs the same way.

The question is a **rank-agreement** question, not a superiority question. It has a
pre-registered decision rule (§2.3) with three branches, only one of which builds anything.

### 1.2 The estimands, stated precisely

Fix a landscape instance `i` drawn from the committed d=6 ensemble, a campaign seed `s`,
and an arm `a`. Let `f_i : [0,1]^6 -> R` be the noiseless oracle, normalised so that
`mu_max = max_x f_i(x) = 1.0` exactly (verified: `results/k6b-conservative.json` carries a
single distinct value of `theta / tau_frac` across all 1,600 rows, equal to `1.0`). Let
`D_{i,s,a}` be the 48 observations the campaign produced and `M_{i,s,a}` the GP fitted to
them.

**Estimand R (simple regret) — the incumbent object.**

```
R(a) = E_{i,s} [ mu_max  -  f_i( x_hat(D_{i,s,a}) ) ]
```

where `x_hat` is the arm's own reported-best rule (`boec.diagnostics.reported_best_curve`
evaluated at truth: pick by what the method saw, score by what was really there — Q17).
This is a functional of **one point**.

**Estimand M (design-space map quality) — K6's primary.** With `G` a fixed 20,000-point
Sobol grid and `tau` a threshold,

```
p_{i,s,a}(x) = P( Y >= tau | x, D_{i,s,a} )                 (posterior predictive)
M(a; gamma, tau) = E_{i,s} [ AUC( p_{i,s,a}(G), 1{ f_i(G) >= tau } ) ]
```

reported alongside the Brier score of the same forecast. This is a functional of **the
whole posterior over the whole box**. It requires no threshold on `p` and is therefore
defined even when the certified region is empty.

**Estimand A (joint certifiability) — K6b's primary.** With `Gamma_i = {x : f_i(x) >= theta}`
the true excursion set and `CE_alpha` the conservative estimate of Azzimonti et al.,

```
alpha*(theta) = sup { alpha in [0,1] : CE_alpha is non-empty }
A(a; theta)   = E_{i,s} [ alpha*_{i,s,a}(theta) ]
```

This is a functional of **the joint posterior law of a random set**. It is bounded below
by `max_x p(x)` and so is always defined, which is why it was chosen over certified volume
(§2.4).

### 1.3 Why R, M and A are different objects, not three views of one

- **R** is invariant to everything the model believes away from `x_hat`. **M** is a global
  ranking quantity over 20,000 points. **A** is a *joint* statement — the probability that
  **no** certified point is false — and 20,000 marginal 95% claims are not one regional
  95% claim.
- R is a *point* functional, M is a *pointwise-marginal* functional integrated over the
  box, and A is a *set* functional. Nothing forces them to agree, and the pre-registered
  hypothesis was that they do not.

**What K6/K6b do NOT estimate.** Certified volume at fixed confidence (withdrawn as
primary before running, §2.4); the cost-assurance curve (withdrawn before running,
Amendment C1); anything at d=8 or at sigma_rel=0.10; anything about a second plate.

### 2.4 THE CENTRAL REGISTRATION DECISION: tau as a fraction, not an absolute

This is the single most consequential thing in the pre-registration and it is worth
setting out in full, because the registration was **changed before running** on the basis
of a closed-form argument, and the change is what stopped the experiment returning a table
of zeros.

**The algebra.** Peterson's `D_gamma` is built on the posterior **predictive**, so it
carries process noise `sigma^2` as well as estimation variance `s^2(x)`:

```
P(Y >= tau | x) = Phi( (mu(x) - tau) / sqrt(s(x)^2 + sigma^2) )
```

Set `s = 0` — infinite data, a perfectly known response surface. Certifying at content
level `gamma` still requires `mu - tau >= z_gamma * sigma`. This repository's noise is
**relative** (`y = f(1 + eps) + eta`), so `sigma(x) = sigma_rel * f(x)` and the noise SD
scales with the mean. Substituting and solving at the optimum gives a closed-form ceiling:

```
tau_max(gamma) = mu_max * (1 - z_gamma * sigma_rel)
```

implemented at `src/boec/designspace.py:70-77`. At `sigma_rel = 0.25`:

| gamma | z (exact inverse normal) | tau_max at s = 0 |
|---|---|---|
| 0.50 | 0.000000 | 1.0000000000 |
| 0.70 | 0.524401 | 0.8688998718 |
| 0.80 | 0.841621 | 0.7895946916 |
| 0.90 | 1.281552 | 0.6796121086 |
| **0.95** | **1.644854** | **0.5887865933** |
| 0.99 | 2.326348 | 0.4184130315 |

(Regenerated; also stored as the `tau_max` column of every row of
`results/k6-designspace.json`.)

**No method certifies above tau = 0.589 at gamma = 0.95 and sigma_rel = 0.25, at any
budget, with any design, ever.** `D_gamma` is floored by *process* noise, which no amount
of data reduces.

**What the earlier draft registered.** Amendment A5 registered absolute
`tau in {0.70, 0.80, 0.85, 0.90}`. **All four exceed 0.589.** Had that grid run, every arm
would have certified nothing at `gamma = 0.95`, the ranking would have been undefined, and
the primary object would have been a table of zeros. Amendment C2 records the catch and the
re-registration:

> A5 registered τ ∈ {0.70, 0.80, 0.85, 0.90}. B0 then proved
> `tau_max = mu_max(1 - z*sigma_rel)` = **0.483–0.589 at γ=0.95**. Every registered τ
> exceeds it, so at γ=0.95 **every arm returns empty at every τ** — a guaranteed table of
> zeros, written into the pre-registration across two amendments without reconciling them.

**Measured outcome of the fix.** AUC is defined in **1,200/1,200 rows for every one of the
eight arms** (`n_auc_nan = 0/1200`, regenerated from the committed JSONs). The primary
metric never degenerated.

**A second, independent reason the fraction is the right coordinate — found after the
fix.** K6b needed the *latent* threshold `theta` corresponding to Peterson's observable
threshold `tau`. Under relative noise:

```
Gamma = {f >= tau + z*sigma} = {f(1 - z*sigma_rel) >= tau} = {f >= tau / (1 - z*sigma_rel)}
```

and substituting `tau = tau_frac * tau_max` with `tau_max = mu_max(1 - z*sigma_rel)`:

```
theta = tau / (1 - z*sigma_rel) = tau_frac * mu_max        for EVERY gamma
```

Verified here over all 24 `(gamma, tau_frac)` combinations: worst
`|theta - tau_frac * mu_max| = 1.110e-16` analytically (one ULP), and `9.07e-11` as
implemented, the difference being that `designspace.tau_max` and the K6 runner each round
to 10 decimal places (`src/boec/designspace.py:77`, `scripts/run_k6_designspace.py:98`).
The registration's phrase *"verified to machine precision"* is correct for the algebra;
the implementation carries an immaterial 1e-10 rounding.

**Consequence, and it is a design fact not a convenience:** the process-noise margin is
*fully absorbed* by the fraction parameterisation, so the latent excursion set depends on
`tau_frac` alone and K6b scores **4 thresholds per campaign, not 24**. `gamma` re-enters
only as interpretation.

**One registration/implementation deviation, recorded.** The K6 registration text writes
`tau_max = mu_max * (1 - z * sigma_rel)` and then separately mentions `s_typical = 0.19`;
Amendment C2 writes `tau_frac ... of tau_max(gamma, s_typical)`. The implemented
`tau_max(gamma, sigma_rel, mu_max=1.0)` takes **no `s` argument** and uses the `s = 0`
ceiling. The absolute thresholds actually run are therefore `tau_frac * mu_max(1 - z*sigma_rel)`,
not `tau_frac * (mu_max - z*sqrt(s_typical^2 + sigma^2))`. This makes the grid *harder*
(higher `tau`) than the C2 reading would, and it is what makes the margin-1 collapse exact.
The deviation is immaterial to the rank question but should not be discovered by a reader.

### 2.5 K6b, as registered (verbatim, commit `a35b1fc`)

> Registered **before `scripts/run_k6b_conservative.py` exists**, and before any K6b number
> is read. Additive to K6; K6 is not modified and its registered primary metric stands.
>
> `{x : LCB(x) >= tau}` is 20,000 **marginal** statements presented as one **regional**

> certified point is false. Prior art: Chevalier (2013) Vorob'ev machinery; Azzimonti,
> Ginsbourger, Chevalier, Bect & Richet (2016; SIAM/ASA JUQ 2021) conservative estimates;
> Chevalier et al. (Technometrics 2014) batch SUR.
>
> * `alpha_star(theta)` — the largest confidence at which a **non-empty** conservative
>   estimate exists. **Always defined**: as `rho -> 1` the Vorob'ev quantile shrinks to the

>   that cannot degenerate the way a fixed-95% volume can.
> * `vorobev_deviation(theta)` — expected symmetric-difference volume; the set-valued
>   analogue of posterior variance, reported beside Brier and AUC.
> * IoU of the **Vorob'ev expectation** against the true excursion set.
> * `|CE_alpha|` at `alpha in {0.50, 0.80, 0.95}`, with **empty counted, never averaged**.

and the limit that travels with every K6b number:

> `CE_alpha` is conservative **given the model**. Hyperparameters are plug-in, so their
> uncertainty sits *outside* the guarantee — Azzimonti et al. flag this themselves. The
> E3-style coverage check is reported beside it to quantify what that costs.

## 3. Methods

### 3.1 Software environment

Python 3.11.15 (recorded in the `provenance` block of every result JSON), torch 2.13.0,
botorch 0.18.1, gpytorch 1.15.2, numpy 2.4.6, scipy 1.17.1. All commands run through
`.venv/bin/python`; there is no bare `python` on this machine.

### 3.2 Campaign regeneration, and why it was necessary

**No file in `results/` stores `X` or `Y`.** `results/e2-grid.json` rows carry
`[arm, auc_post_init, best, dim, instance, regret, seed, sigma]` and nothing else (verified:
`sorted(rows[0].keys())`). `Campaign.state_dict` has always persisted
`train_X`/`train_Y`/`train_Yvar`; the E2 runners simply never called `save()`.

`src/boec/replay.py:regenerate(instance, dim, sigma, seed, arm)` therefore re-runs the
campaign deterministically from its five-tuple key and returns a `CampaignRecord` carrying
`X`, `Y`, `Yvar`, `regret`, `optimum_value` and `kept_factors`. Three implementation points
are load-bearing:

1. **`Yvar` is recomputed from the stored `Y`, never by re-evaluating.** The DoE arm returns
   `X_visited`/`Y_visited` but no variance. Calling `evaluator.evaluate(X)` again draws
   *fresh noise* and would hand the model a different plug-in variance from the one the
   campaign carried. `replay.py:188-189` calls
   `boec.torch_oracle._plug_in_yvar(Y_stored, sigma_rel, sigma_add)` instead.
   `_plug_in_yvar` is a function of the **noisy reading**, so assumed precision is
   correlated with the residual — a documented coupling, asserted by
   `tests/test_replay.py::test_regenerate_yvar_is_coupled_to_the_reading_not_the_truth`
   at `corr(|Y|, Yvar) > 0.9`.
2. **`kept_factors` is recorded, not inferred.** `DoEResult.X_visited` is `(48, d)` with all
   `d` columns present, and the 20-run screen varies every factor; only stage 2 pins the
   dropped ones. A "zero-variance column" heuristic would therefore find nothing and would
   silently certify a factor the response-surface fit never saw move.
   `tests/test_replay.py::test_doe_arm_reports_which_factors_its_stage_two_varied` and
   `::test_adaptive_arms_report_no_screen` assert both halves.
3. **The spread arms reproduce `run_e2.static_curve`'s *arithmetic*, not merely its result.**
   `static_curve` averages `N_ORDERINGS = 20` best-so-far curves whose terminal values are
   all identical (the final value is order-invariant), and the float64 mean of 20 copies of
   `x` is not bitwise `x`. **Measured here over the 150 committed d=6, sigma=0.25 spread
   rows: computing a single curve misses the committed column by up to `5.551e-16`
   (about 5 ULP); reproducing the 20-ordering average misses it by `0.000e+00`.** The fix
   was to match the arithmetic, so the gate stays exact and no tolerance was invented.
   (`docs/FINDINGS-SPADE.md` §5 states this artefact as `1.110e-16` and the `replay.py`
   docstring as "up to 5.6e-17"; all three are the same order and none is in a committed
   results JSON — the committed evidence is the zero gate-failure count.)

to `0.0` for them — i.e. they were also gated at exact equality, by fallback rather than by
measurement, and passed.

**Gate failures across all four production runs: 0** (`gate_failures` array empty in all
four JSONs; 800 gated campaign comparisons in total).

### 3.4 The surrogate

`boec.surrogate.build_gp(X, Y, Yvar, bounds)` — BoTorch `SingleTaskGP` with:

- **`train_Yvar` supplied as known**, in the original units, per observation. The model is
  never asked to infer a noise level. The docstring enumerates five silent
  misconfigurations this function exists to prevent, two of which concern `Yvar` units and
  rescaling.
- explicit `bounds` (never inferred from data), `Normalize` input transform, `Standardize`
  outcome transform,
- `kernel_structure="product"` by default — the kernel E2 ran, and the default is not
  changed here. Q30's arms pass `"additive"` and `"additive+interaction"` through
  `replay.KERNEL_ARMS`.
- MAP fitting via `fit_gpytorch_mll` on the exact marginal log-likelihood. **Hyperparameters
  are plug-in point estimates**, which is the stated limit on every K6b number (§2.5, §6.3).

### 3.5 The evaluation grid, and the quadratic-posterior trap

`boec.norms.sobol_grid(6, 20_000, seed=0)` — scrambled Sobol, deterministic in the seed
(`tests/test_norms.py::test_sobol_grid_is_deterministic_in_its_seed`). The same grid is
used for every arm, instance and seed, so all contrasts are paired on the grid as well as
on `(instance, seed)`.

**`model.posterior(X)` builds the joint covariance over all of `X`, and only the per-point
marginals are ever used.** Measured on this repository's d=6 GP and recorded in
`src/boec/designspace.py:84-98`:

```
N =  2,000  ->   0.06 s
N = 20,000  -> 100.60 s        (a 10x grid costs ~1,700x the time; 3.2 GB dense)
```

`gp_adapter` chunks at `POSTERIOR_CHUNK = 2048`, turning the 20,000-point grid into ~0.6 s.
`tests/test_designspace.py::test_gp_adapter_chunking_gives_the_same_answer_as_one_shot`
asserts the chunked and one-shot answers agree. `boec.norms._mean_and_truth` checks for the
`posterior_mean_and_sd` protocol **first**, specifically so that a caller who has already
paid for a chunked posterior is not silently routed back into `model.posterior(X_grid)`.

The K6 runner computes `mean, sd` **once** per campaign and wraps them in a frozen `_M`
stand-in, so all 24 `(gamma, tau_frac)` cells for a campaign share one posterior evaluation.

### 3.6 The K6 deliverables

Per campaign, per `(gamma, tau_frac)` — 24 cells, all combinations, no reduction:

- `tau = round(tau_frac * tau_max(gamma, sigma_rel), 10)`.
- **Predictive map (PRIMARY).**
  `predictive_probability_map(m, G, tau, sigma_pred) = Phi((mu - tau)/sqrt(s^2 + sigma_pred^2))`
  where `sigma_pred = sqrt((sigma_rel * mu)^2 + sigma_add^2)` is a **plug-in from the
  posterior mean**, because a lab does not know `f` (`scripts/run_k6_designspace.py:87`).
  Passing a scalar here would silently answer a homoscedastic question the campaigns never
  asked.
- **Latent map (SECONDARY).** `probability_map(m, G, tau) = Phi((mu - tau)/s)`. Retained
  because E3 measured the latent 95% interval at 0.7644 coverage (worst cell, d=8
  sigma=0.25; `results/e3.log`, RESULTS.md §E3) while the predictive interval recovers to
  ~0.90–0.92, so the gap between the two maps is a result in its own right.
- `D_gamma = {p_pred >= gamma}`; `latent = {p_lat >= gamma}`; `true_set = {f >= tau}`.
- `brier_and_auc(p, truth, tau)` for both maps. **AUC returns `nan` when one class is
  absent**, never 0.5, so an undefined value cannot average into a mean as if it were data
  (`tests/test_designspace.py::test_auc_is_nan_when_one_class_is_absent_not_half`).
- `iou`, `false_inclusion_rate` — both return `nan` for an empty region, never 0.0, because
  0.0 would read as perfectly safe when the honest answer is that nothing was claimed.
- `inscribed_box_from_mask(G, D_gamma, active=active, seed_score=p_pred)` — the largest
  axis-aligned box lying entirely inside `D_gamma`, by greedy expansion at step `1/20`.
  `active` implements Amendment B3(a).
- `sup_err` and `grid_r2` from `boec.norms`, on the same grid. `grid_r2` is **deliberately
  not clamped at zero**.

### 3.7 The K6b deliverables

Per campaign, per `tau_frac` — 4 thresholds, `theta = tau_frac * mu_max` (§2.4):

- **Conditional simulation, not orthant probabilities.** `joint_draws` takes
  `model.posterior(X_sub).mvn.covariance_matrix`, adds `JITTER = 1e-8 * I`, Choleskys it,
  and draws `N_DRAWS = 512` joint samples with a `torch.Generator` seeded by the campaign
  seed. Azzimonti et al. compute containment with orthant probabilities; this is a Monte
  Carlo approximation of the same object.
- **Why a 2,000-point subset.** `SUBSET_N = 2_000`, Sobol seed 0 — a subset of the same
  sequence K6 uses. The joint covariance is **3.2 GB at 20,000 points**; at 2,000 the joint
  draw plus Cholesky plus samples costs **0.14 s** (measured; recorded in
  `src/boec/vorobev.py:53-61` and `scripts/run_k6b_conservative.py:22-25`). The measured
  0.14 s figure was taken at 256 samples; the production runs use 512.
- `excursion_probability(draws, theta) = mean_j 1{draw_j(x) >= theta}` — the coverage
  function `p(x)`, quantised to multiples of `1/512 = 0.001953`.
- `vorobev_quantile(p, rho) = {p >= rho}`; `vorobev_expectation` chooses the quantile whose
  volume matches `E[vol(Gamma)]` by scanning achievable volumes, so the returned set is a
  genuine quantile rather than an interpolation.
- `containment_probability(draws, mask, theta)` — the **joint** quantity: the fraction of
  posterior draws in which *every* point of `mask` clears `theta`. The empty set is
  trivially contained and returns 1.0; `alpha_star` and `conservative_estimate` exclude it
  explicitly.
- `alpha_star(draws, theta, n_rho=64)` — scans 64 equally spaced `rho` in [0,1], takes the
  best containment over non-empty quantiles, and returns `max(best, max_x p(x))`.
- `conservative_estimate(draws, theta, alpha, n_rho=64)` — the **largest** Vorob'ev quantile
  whose containment is `>= alpha`; an all-false mask when none reaches `alpha`.
- `vorobev_deviation(draws, theta)` — expected symmetric-difference volume between the
  random set and its Vorob'ev expectation.
- Per `alpha in {0.50, 0.80, 0.95}`: `ce_vol`, `ce_empty`, `ce_false_in` (against truth),
  `ce_contain` (from the draws).

### 3.8 Statistics

Fixed by the registration and by Q20 §2, and implemented identically in both analysis
scripts:

- **Unit of analysis:** the `(instance, seed)` pair. 25 instances x 2 seeds = **n = 50**
  for every contrast. Pairs with `nan` on either side drop, and the dropped count is
  printed; for every AUC contrast reported here, `dropped = 0`.
- **Magnitude:** mean paired difference with a **percentile bootstrap CI**, 4,000
  resamples of the 50 paired differences, `numpy.random.default_rng(0)`, 2.5/97.5
  percentiles.
- **Yes/no:** two-sided **Wilcoxon signed-rank** (`scipy.stats.wilcoxon`, default settings;
  `ValueError` on all-zero differences is mapped to `p = 1.0`).
- **Disagreements between the two are reported, not resolved** (Q20 §2). Several occur; see
  §5.8 and §6.7.
- **Multiplicity.** The registration cites Q39 (Holm across cells, since none of the 24 is
  a registered primary). **Neither committed analysis script applies Holm.** Holm-adjusted
  counts are computed and reported here alongside the raw counts wherever they change the
  reading (§5.4, §5.5).

### 5.6 A registered prediction of ours was wrong, in the opposite direction — RETRACTED

**What was predicted.** `docs/SPADE-SPEC.md` §"Honest probabilities" carries:

**Why the prediction was wrong.** E3 measures **pointwise coverage of the latent response

on one side or the other. A model can have 76% pointwise coverage and near-zero false
inclusion simultaneously, because the two statistics integrate different events. E3 was
over-read.

predictive map for every arm (`doe` 0.0477 vs 0.0046; `qlognei` 0.0026 vs 0.0000). The gap
between the two maps was registered as a result in its own right, and it exists; it just
does not reach the nominal 0.05 for either map.

regions only, and 54%–75% of the rows are empty. The arms with the most empty regions
(`sobol`, 149/200) are exactly those whose surviving regions are smallest and safest, so

|---|---|
| 0.50 | 0.5595 (doe) – 0.5998 (sobol) |

| 0.95 | **0.9605 (sobol) – 0.9980 (doe)** |

**The mechanism is identified and it is a known, registered failure mode.**
`scripts/run_k6b_conservative.py` never reads `kept_factors` (§2.8). The `doe` GP is fitted
on data whose 2 screened axes have no stage-2 variation, so along those axes the likelihood
is flat, the ARD lengthscale reverts toward the prior mode 0.5016, and the posterior
uncertainty is set by a BoTorch default rather than by data. The conditional simulation
then draws a joint field whose covariance on those axes is prior-driven, and the resulting
"conservative" estimate certifies points it has no evidence about. This is exactly
Amendment A3 option **(c)** — *"happens silently; not defensible in a batch record"* — and
K6b ran it because Amendment B3's option (a) was implemented only in K6.

**Therefore the correct statement of the K6b safety result is:**

> With plug-in hyperparameters and 48 wells, the conservative excursion estimate achieves
> at or above its nominal joint confidence for every arm that varies all six factors, at
> alpha in {0.50, 0.80, 0.95}. It **fails** its nominal level at all three alphas for the
> screened classical arm, whose posterior on two of six axes is prior-driven. The
> containment statistic reported in `results/k6b-analysis.json` is computed in-sample and
> cannot detect this; the failure is visible only against ground truth.

This both strengthens and qualifies the screening finding of §5.3: screening does not merely
cost map quality, it **breaks the joint certification guarantee** for the region the
screened arm does certify.

## 6. Threats to validity

Ordered roughly by how much they would change a conclusion.

### 6.1 K6 and K6b tested plate 1 only, and SPADE is a two-round method

`docs/SPADE-SPEC.md` Stage 5 specifies a second plate, and its own text says *"the
certificate is 2 rounds by default; one round is the map, not the batch record."* Version A
— the version scored here — **changes no sampling at all**; it re-scores campaigns that
already existed under a new set of metrics.

Running A first was the registered sequencing (Amendment B1: *"A · map only ... build when:
now — it is K6"*) and was correct as a cheap kill test. But:

- *"A one-shot spread design does not map better than qLogNEI"* is supported by these data.
- *"SPADE does not map better than qLogNEI"* is **not tested**, because plate 2 (batch LSE
  on the `D_gamma` boundary) is precisely the machinery designed to repair a weak certified
  region, and it has not been run in any committed file.

Everything in §5 should be read as a statement about **plate 1**.

### 6.2 Acquisition-function dependence — the result flips between qLogEI and qLogNEI

`lhs` beats qLogEI in 9 of 24 cells and never loses; `lhs` loses to qLogNEI in 15 of 24.
**The headline verdict on "does a spread design map better than adaptive search" is
determined by which acquisition is in the comparison.**

This is the failure mode Q57 documented and this project has already been burned by once.
Q57 (RESULTS.md §Q57 Result 3) explicitly retracted a Q55 sentence — *"the classical arm
tested materially better conditions and the noisy readout hid it entirely"* at d=8,
sigma=0.10 — because that contrast was null against qLogNEI: *"The effect was a property of
qLogEI's search, not of the classical design, and the claim is withdrawn."*

Had K6 been run with `qlogei` alone (which is what the arm list at registration time
effectively was, since the spread arms had not been added and `qlogei-add`/`qlogei-addonly`
are both qLogEI variants), the same trap would have fired again. It did not fire only
because `qlognei` happened to be in the E2 arm list. **Any statement in §5.4 must name the
acquisition.**

Note also that all three of `qlogei`, `qlogei-add`, `qlogei-addonly` use the same
acquisition, so the eight-arm ranking contains four qLogEI-family entries, one qLogNEI, one
screened classical, and three space-filling designs. The "8 arms" are not eight independent
methods.

### 6.3 Plug-in hyperparameters sit outside the `CE_alpha` guarantee

Azzimonti et al.'s conservative-estimate result is conditional on the GP. Here the kernel
hyperparameters are MAP point estimates from `fit_gpytorch_mll` with no marginalisation, so
their uncertainty is not represented in the joint draws. The registration carries this as a
stated limit and Azzimonti et al. flag it themselves.

The out-of-sample check in §5.10.2 quantifies the cost and it is not uniform: for the seven
arms that vary all six factors the plug-in approximation is adequate at this budget; for
the screened arm it is not, and the failure is severe. The correct reading is not *"plug-in
hyperparameters are fine"* but *"plug-in hyperparameters are fine where the design informs
every lengthscale, and are not where one does not"*.

The K1 registration proposed replicate-identified `sigma` as a partial repair; that run
(K1 noise-ceiling) has not been executed.

### 6.4 One oracle family, one dimension, one noise level

Everything in §5 is `d = 6`, `sigma_rel = 0.25`, the biphasic Hill ensemble, 25 instances x
2 seeds. Specifically **not** covered:

- **d = 8**, where the E2 tables show BO doing relatively better and where the DoE arm's
  screen is structurally identical (`STAGE1_FRACTION[8] = 4`, same 16+4 screen, same 4
  survivors, same 27-run CCD).
- **sigma_rel = 0.10.** This matters more than usual here, because `tau_max` depends on
  `sigma_rel` directly: at `sigma_rel = 0.10, gamma = 0.95` the ceiling is 0.8355, not
  0.5889, so the entire threshold grid moves and the emptiness structure of §5.7 would be
  different. Amendment A5 registered a sigma sweep over {0.25, 0.20, 0.15, 0.10}; **it has
  not been run**, and it noted that committed campaigns exist only at {0.25, 0.10}, so
  0.20 and 0.15 would be new, ungatable, exploratory campaigns.
- **Other landscape families.** Q42/Q53 established that this project's spread-versus-BO
  results are landscape-shaped — `spread_gp` ties on smooth families and loses on deceptive
  ones. None of hartmann6, ackley, levy or rosenbrock was scored under K6's metrics. The
  Hill oracle is biphasic and coordinate-wise unimodal, which is the regime SPADE's own
  Stage 3 says it is *supposed* to win in.

### 6.5 The anti-conservatism prediction was retracted, and the retraction has a lesson

Covered in full at §5.6. Restated as a threat: **a probability was raised from 0.75 to 0.9
on the strength of a statistic (E3's pointwise latent coverage, 0.7644) that does not
measure the predicted quantity (false inclusion in a thresholded region).** The measurement
came out conservative by 10x–500x. The general failure is using a *pointwise marginal
calibration* statistic to forecast a *set-membership error rate*; the two integrate
different events over the grid.

| 0.75 | 0.2894 | 0.5961 | 0.7541 | 0.9001 | 0.9609 | **0.9962** |
| 0.85 | 0.0684 | 0.3236 | 0.5368 | 0.7863 | 0.9113 | **0.9909** |
| 0.95 | **0.0029** | 0.1075 | 0.2891 | 0.6137 | 0.8234 | 0.9809 |

At `gamma = 0.99, tau_frac = 0.60` the minority class is about **16 grid points out of
20,000**; at `gamma = 0.50, tau_frac = 0.95` it is about **59**. AUC is still *defined*
(both classes are present in every one of the 1,200 rows per arm), but in those corners it
is estimated from a few dozen minority-class points and its sampling variability is far
larger than in the balanced cells. **The "24 cells" are not 24 comparably informative
cells**, and treating "0 of 24 agree" as 24 independent pieces of evidence overstates the
evidence. The cells are also strongly dependent: they share the same 50 campaigns, the same
grid and the same posterior.

Related: because `tau = tau_frac * tau_max(gamma)` and `tau_max` falls with `gamma`, the
high-`gamma` rows use *low absolute thresholds* (0.251 to 0.418 at `gamma = 0.99`).
Certifying a threshold that 99% of the box already exceeds is close to vacuous as a
manufacturing statement. This is the unavoidable other side of the fix in §2.4: making the
grid feasible at high assurance necessarily makes it easy.

### 6.7 The two registered statistics disagree, and `alpha*` is quantised

`alpha*` is a Monte Carlo quantity built from 512 binary draws, so it takes values on a
grid of multiples of `1/512 = 0.001953` (verified: all 1,600 committed `alpha_star` values
are exact multiples of 1/512, with 439 distinct values observed). Near the ceiling it is
heavily tied — `doe` at `tau_frac = 0.60` is `0.9998 +/- 0.001`, and 6 of its 50 paired
differences against `qlogei` are exactly zero.

Wilcoxon signed-rank drops zero differences and ranks the rest; a percentile bootstrap of
the mean does not. In this regime they come apart, and §5.8 shows seven cells where they
do — including two (`doe` - `qlogei` and `doe` - `qlognei` at `tau_frac = 0.95`) where the
bootstrap CI excludes zero while `p` is 0.38 and 0.73. Q20 §2 requires reporting the
disagreement rather than choosing; that is done, but a reader should not treat either
column as decisive in those cells.

A second consequence: 512 draws bound the resolution of `alpha*` at ~0.002, and several
reported contrasts (e.g. `random` - `lhs` at `tau_frac = 0.95`, mean -0.0002) are at or
below one quantum.

### 6.8 The K6b containment statistic is in-sample

Covered at §5.10.1. Restated: `ce_contain_alpha` cannot fall below `alpha`, so it is
incapable of falsifying the guarantee it appears to verify. The committed
`results/k6b-analysis.json` presents it under the heading *"CE_alpha emptiness and ACTUAL
containment (nominal vs achieved)"*, which invites exactly the wrong reading. The
out-of-sample statistic (`ce_false_in_alpha` against truth) is in the same JSON and is the
one that carries information.

### 6.9 The spread arms are post-hoc with respect to the registration

Established at §2.1. `lhs`, `sobol` and `random` were added to `boec.replay` at 22:34:00,
after the K6 main run completed and after the commit `38770e7` "K6 complete: the
design-space ranking diverges from regret in 24 of 24 cells". They appear in no
registration.

In mitigation: (i) all three arms have committed `e2-grid.json` columns, so their campaigns
are gated exactly and were not tuned; (ii) their designs are fixed by `seed` and were not
selected; (iii) the metric, grid and statistics were all fixed before they were added, and
`analyse_k6.py` asserts configuration agreement before merging. Against: the *choice to add
them at all* was made after seeing that the registered arm list could not answer the
registered question, and the strongest headline in the study (§5.3, 24/24) exists only
because of that choice. It should be reported as a post-hoc analysis of pre-existing data,
not as a pre-registered contrast.

### 6.10 The primary metric is ambiguous between AUC and Brier

The registration names *"Brier score and AUC of the probability map ... plus IoU of
`D_gamma`"* — three quantities. **`scripts/analyse_k6.py` ranks on AUC alone and reports
neither Brier nor IoU** (verified by grep: neither string appears outside a docstring). Both
are computed and committed per row in `results/k6-designspace.json`; neither reaches the
analysis. §5.2 shows AUC and Brier disagree on the winner (qLogNEI best AUC 0.7392 and only
sixth-best Brier; `sobol` best Brier 0.1273 and third mean AUC rank), so which of the three
registered quantities is consulted changes the answer. Amendment A5 further registered a
Murphy calibration–refinement decomposition of Brier with 10 equal-count bins; **no
committed file contains it.** Since calibration is the component E3's result bears on, the
missing decomposition is the missing half of the registered primary.

### 6.11 Two arms are ungated

`qlogei-add` and `qlogei-addonly` have no column in `results/e2-grid.json`. They were run
through `replay.regenerate` with `kernel_structure` overrides and were **not** compared to
any committed value. Amendment A1 specified *"Gate them against `results/q30-additive.json`
under the Task 1 policy"*; the K6 runner's `committed` dictionary is built from
`e2-grid.json` only, so `committed.get(...)` returns `None` for these arms and the gate is
skipped silently. Their 2,400 K6 rows and 400 K6b rows are therefore ungated, and §5.5's
A1 conclusion rests on ungated campaigns.

### 6.12 The `doe` inscribed-box volumes are not comparable across arms

`inscribed_box_from_mask` returns `prod(widths[active])`. For `doe`, `active.sum() == 4` in
1,200/1,200 rows; for every other arm it is 6. So the committed `box_vol_pred` column mixes
4-dimensional and 6-dimensional volumes with no units column. At `gamma = 0.95,
tau_frac = 0.75` the means are `doe` 8.400e-01 (a 4-volume) against 2.5e-04 to 4.5e-04 (a
6-volume) for the rest. **Comparing those numbers directly is a dimensional error**, and it
is available to anyone reading the JSON. Neither analysis script reports `box_vol_pred`,
which is why the error has not propagated, but the column is committed.

### 6.13 Grid discretisation

`D_gamma`, `Gamma`, IoU, volumes and the inscribed box are all computed on a fixed
20,000-point Sobol grid in 6 dimensions — roughly 5.2 points per axis if it were a lattice.
`inscribed_box_from_mask` expands in steps of `1/20` per axis and requires every grid point
inside the proposed box to be certified, so a box can be blocked by a single grid point and
its volume is a discrete approximation. `alpha*`, containment and the Vorob'ev quantities
are computed on a 2,000-point subset, 10x coarser again. No convergence study in grid size
exists in any committed file.

### 6.14 The `sigma_pred` plug-in

The predictive map uses `sigma_pred = sqrt((sigma_rel * mu)^2 + sigma_add^2)` with `mu` the
posterior mean, because a lab does not know `f`. This is the honest choice, but it makes
the predictive map's noise term a function of the model's own (mis-scaled, §5.2) mean. For
`doe`, whose `grid_r2` is -6.19, the plug-in `sigma_pred` is correspondingly wrong. Part of
the predictive-versus-latent gap reported as a result is therefore an artefact of the
plug-in for that arm.

## 7. Unexplained observations

Recorded because an unexplained result that favours a hypothesis is as dangerous as one
that does not. Neither of these is predicted by any argument in `docs/SPADE-SPEC.md`,
`docs/ODIN-SPEC.md` or the plan.

### 7.1 `random` places 2nd or 3rd on `alpha*` at three of four thresholds

`random` is 4th at `tau_frac = 0.60`, **2nd** at 0.75, **2nd** at 0.85 and **3rd** at 0.95
(§5.8) — ahead of both BO arms and ahead of `lhs` at two thresholds. It does this while
having by far the **worst simple regret** (0.2216, last of eight) and the **second-worst
mean AUC rank** (6.75 of 8).

An arm that is worst at finding the optimum and near-worst at mapping the box is
second-best at stating a joint certificate. No mechanism in the specs predicts this. Two
hypotheses, neither tested: (i) `alpha*` at the easier thresholds rewards having *some*
region of high, spatially coherent exceedance probability, which unstructured sampling may
supply as readily as stratified sampling; (ii) `random`'s designs have higher variance in
local density, so the best-covered neighbourhood in a random design may be denser than in
an LHS of the same size. **Both are speculation and neither is offered as an explanation.**

### 7.2 `sobol` places last on `alpha*` at all four thresholds

`sobol` ranks **8th of 8 at all four thresholds** in `results/k6b-analysis.json`
(`alpha*` = 0.9480, 0.5275, 0.1746, 0.0355). It also has the highest emptiness of any arm
at every alpha (64%/79%/94%) and the smallest non-empty `CE_0.95` (1.5 points of 2,000).

(`docs/FINDINGS-SPADE.md` §4.8 says "last at three of four"; the committed JSON says four
of four. See §8.8.)

This is directly counter-intuitive. `sobol` is a low-discrepancy sequence — it should be
the *best*-covering of the three space-filling arms — and on the K6 map it is: mean AUC
rank 2.96 (3rd of 8, ahead of `lhs` at 4.21), best Brier of any arm (0.1273), and
statistically indistinguishable from qLogNEI in all 24 cells. **The same arm is 3rd on map
quality and last on joint certifiability.**

Observations that constrain any explanation, without providing one:

- `sobol` also has the *lowest* Vorob'ev deviation at `tau_frac = 0.95` and a middling IoU,
  so it is not simply "more uncertain everywhere".
- `sobol` beats `lhs` on AUC in 7 of 24 cells and loses in 3, but loses to `lhs` on
  `alpha*` at all four thresholds (-0.0102, -0.1016, -0.1127, -0.0443).
- `sobol` is the one spread arm that shares the 14-point opening batch with the BO arms
  (`initial_design` *is* a Sobol prefix, so pairing is free); `lhs` is exempt from pairing.
  Whether that changes the effective design is untested.

Flagged as unexplained. It is on the follow-up list in `docs/FINDINGS-SPADE.md` §7 item 5
and remains open.

## 10. What may and may not be written from these data

**May be written.**

- At `d = 6, sigma_rel = 0.25`, on the biphasic Hill ensemble, at 48 wells: the ranking of
  eight campaign designs by AUC of Peterson's predictive design-space map disagrees with
  their ranking by simple regret in **24 of 24** `(gamma, tau_frac)` cells, and their
  ranking by `alpha*` disagrees in **4 of 4** thresholds.
- Between two non-adaptive 48-well arms differing only in a 6->4 screen, a stage-2 sub-box
  and sequencing, the unscreened arm is better on map AUC by **+0.105 to +0.221 in 24 of 24
  cells (all Holm-significant)** while being **worse** on regret by
  **+0.0312 [+0.0133,+0.0491], p = 0.0028**. Post-hoc with respect to the registration
  (§6.9).
- Against ground truth, the conservative excursion estimate meets its nominal joint
  confidence at alpha in {0.50, 0.80, 0.95} for every arm that varies all six factors, and
  **fails at all three levels for the screened classical arm** whose posterior on two axes
  is prior-driven.
- Doubling surrogate accuracy via an additive kernel is null on regret, and is not
  demonstrated to help on map quality or `alpha*` under the registered multiplicity
  control, despite being positive in direction in 24 of 24 map cells.
- Our own registered prediction that nominal-95% certified regions would be materially
  anti-conservative is **retracted**; measured false inclusion is 10x–500x below the 0.05
  target.

**May not be written.**

- Anything about SPADE as specified. Only plate 1 has been run (§6.1).
- Anything about a spread design beating adaptive search without naming the acquisition
  (§6.2).
- Anything at `d = 8`, at `sigma_rel != 0.25`, or on any landscape family other than Hill.
- *"The conservative estimate is genuinely conservative"* without the screened-arm exception
  (§5.10.2), and never on the basis of the in-sample `ce_contain` column (§5.10.1).
- Any comparison of `box_vol_pred` across arms (§6.12).
- Any rounds claim. Rounds are reported nowhere in K6 or K6b, and every contrast here is at
  equal wells only (§4).
