The Savings Curve That Isn’t

We set out to measure how many experiments Bayesian optimization saves against a classical DoE pipeline. Under the analysis registered before the run, that number does not exist at any target. Here is what the curves actually look like, and the one comparison that survived.

The classical arm is censored above 50 % at every rule-C target — including the loosest. A savings ratio needs both arms to arrive. Neither a median nor a mean is defined anywhere on this curve.

Registered in advance: above 50 % censoring, report the rate and no point estimate; a median over only the runs that arrived is biased. That gate is why this page is a null rather than a headline — and a first pass that bypassed it did produce one, which was retracted.

Why there is no curve

Each cell is one target regret. Shading is the share of the 25 landscapes where that arm never reached the target inside the 200-evaluation cap. Anything past the halfway mark is hatched: the registered rule forbids a point estimate there.

The classical doe row is blocked across its whole length. Its unconstrained rule-C regret is 0.4163 — worse than the loosest target in the set, so it cannot arrive anywhere. random has no rule-C column at all: it carries no model, so it has no recommendation to score.

The cost curves

Median regret against evaluations spent; lower is better. Bands are the interquartile range across landscapes. The classical arm sits on a coarser grid on purpose — it only yields a recommendation when a whole 48-run pipeline completes, so it has four points where the others have eleven. Plotting it on their grid would invent readings it never produced.

Read the picture against the statistics, not instead of them. At budget 48 the paired contrast between qlogei and spread_gp is null on both rules at both noise levels — every interval covers zero. The visible gap between those two lines is not a measured win.

The same data, priced in rounds

Evaluations are not what a lab pays. A round is a plate cycle — you plate, incubate, stain, read, and only then choose what to try next. Here the x-axis is rounds of waiting rather than wells consumed. Nothing about the measurements changed; only the price did.

Spending all 200 evaluations costs spread_gp 1 round, doe 12, and qlogei 48. That is the axis on which these methods genuinely differ — and it is a property of the batch structure, fixed in advance, not a result of the experiment.

The one result that survives

Everything above conditions on arriving, and arriving is what the classical arm mostly fails to do. Arrival itself is unconditioned — all 25 landscapes contribute, nothing is selected — so it is the one quantity this grid measures cleanly. Paired per landscape, exact binomial on the discordant pairs.

At the optimistic assay, BO reaches a regret of 0.10 in 24 of 25 landscapes where the classical pipeline reaches it in 13. Cost among those arrivals: 32 wells vs 48. Time: 6 rounds vs 3. BO arrives more often, cheaper in wells, slower in rounds. A one-shot spread+GP matches the 24/25 rate and the 32-well median in 1 round. Rounds were reconstructed from evaluation checkpoints, not logged. Only this cell survives Holm over ten arrival tests. At the realistic assay the rate difference vanishes.

So the honest sentence is not “BO gets there in fewer experiments” — that comparison is undefined as a savings ratio — but “at a quiet assay, BO gets there more often, in fewer wells and more plate cycles.” Cost = wells. Time = rounds. The rate difference disappears at the noise this project registered as primary. A one-shot spread+GP matches the 24/25 rate and 32-well median in 1 round.

What would make these curves wrong

  • The classical arm never moves its design region. It repeats a fixed 48-run pipeline with a fresh seed and keeps the best. Textbook sequential RSM inserts a steepest-ascent phase that walks the design toward the optimum. Every ratio here is therefore biased in BO’s favour — conceded in the registration, not after the numbers landed.
  • The loose targets measure granularity, not adaptivity. qLogEI opens with 14 points, so at n=8 and n=12 it has made zero adaptive decisions. Any apparent advantage there is checkpoint spacing.
  • Each row is a different subset of landscapes. As the target tightens, n falls from 25 toward 3, so reading a trend down a column is partly survivorship.
  • One campaign seed per landscape, where the main grid used two.
  • d=8 was not run — declared out of scope, not attempted and dropped.
