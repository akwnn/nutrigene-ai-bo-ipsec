SPADE: scoring the design-space map, not just the setpoint, in matched-budget experimental design

What this paper is not. It is not a claim that Bayesian optimization (BO) versus classical response-surface methodology (RSM/DoE) is a new question — a companion manuscript (Yung, 2026, "Matched-budget benchmarking of Bayesian optimization and response-surface methodology depends on the terminal decision rule") already ran that comparison on a matched 48-well budget and reported the finding this paper is built on: which method "wins" reverses depending on the terminal decision rule used to read a single recommended recipe off the same campaign. It is not a wet-lab validation, not a claim that a two-plate protocol is architecturally novel (sequential-design and space-filling-plus-adaptive-batch literatures already exist), and not a claim that SPADE beats Sobol, BO or DoE in general — this project's own prospective study explicitly forbids that conclusion and the results below do not support it.

What this paper is. A demonstration that the terminal-rule reversal in the companion study is a symptom of scoring the wrong object: a single recommended point. SPADE (Single-Plate Assured Design Envelope, extended to two plates) proposes scoring the *design-space map* — a probability-of-acceptability surface and a certified operating region — as a co-primary deliverable alongside point regret, decomposed into map error, calibration and refinement rather than collapsed into one scalar. This paper reports what that reframing buys: map quality and point regret are shown to be different objects that different methods win (0 of 18 rankable cells agree, K6/K6b); a Murphy calibration/refinement decomposition shows SPADE buys sharpness, not reliability (P7); a cross-family, cross-γ certificate audit narrows exactly where the certificate can be trusted (P8, F3); and a pre-registered, prospective (not re-scored) confirmatory study with 99,601 combined rows across seven conditions (`spade-final-2026-08-23`, KF-1–KF-10) tests, for the first time in this project, whether the specific boundary-targeting mechanism that chooses SPADE's second plate earns its complexity — and finds that it does not, at the registered budget, even after two independently engineered repair attempts (`spade-kf3-followup-2026-08-24`).

Abstract

Background. A companion study (Yung, 2026) found that under a matched 48-well budget on a synthetic Hill-type biphasic benchmark, whether Bayesian optimization (BO) or classical DoE "wins" reverses depending on which terminal decision rule is used to read a single recommended recipe off the campaign: DoE leads under measured-value argmax at the noisier condition (−0.0595, d=6, σ=0.25), BO leads by a wide margin under naive unconstrained model recommendation (+0.27 to +0.36, mostly an artefact of extrapolating a saddle-shaped quadratic fit off its own design region), and the two are statistically indistinguishable under in-region/ridge recommendation (null in 3 of 4 cells) or after a three-well confirmation protocol (−0.0009, null). That reversal motivates the present question: if the choice of terminal rule can flip a benchmark's headline result, is scoring a single recommended point the right object at all? SPADE reframes the deliverable as a design-space map — a probability-of-acceptability surface and a certified operating region, computed from a two-plate protocol (Plate 1: 40–48 wells, space-filling; Plate 2: 8 wells, reactive) — and asks whether that map, not a point, is a better-behaved and more decision-relevant scalar.

Methods. Plate 1 is a plain Latin-hypercube design (the founding specification's 49-point strength-2 orthogonal-array LHS with covariate adjustment was never implemented; every result below uses plain LHS, and this gap is treated as a first-class limitation, not a footnote). Plate 2 places 8 wells by a boundary SUR/straddle-targeting rule (`spade_cf_m0`), with two variants that additionally spend wells on local exploitation inside the certified region (`m4`, `m8`). A conservative excursion certificate is computed from the joint GP posterior using cross-fit (split-draw) containment at N_DRAWS=4096, replacing an earlier same-draw estimator shown to be anti-conservative below 1,024 draws (F3). Map quality is scored by symmetric-difference error volume (Azzimonti & Ginsbourger decomposition) after AUC was found to be blind to a negative-R² fitted surface (K6/K6b); reliability is scored by a Murphy calibration/refinement decomposition of the Brier score (P7), reported as two separate numbers, never collapsed. The certificate is audited cross-family (Hill, Levy, Rosenbrock, Ackley, Hartmann6) and cross-γ (P8). A pre-registered prospective confirmatory study (`spade-final-2026-08-23`, commit c4f58d3) runs fresh — not re-scored — campaigns across seven conditions (one TARGET: hill, d=6, σ=0.10; six ROBUSTNESS/EXCEPTION) with a mandatory 13-arm registry, N_DRAWS=4096 cross-fit, four Holm families and ten pre-registered kill conditions (KF-1–KF-10) adjudicating whether targeted Plate-2 placement earns its complexity over a random-placement control (`spade_random_plate2`) and a plate-1-only control (`spade_plate1_only`). A follow-up study (`spade-kf3-followup-2026-08-24`) tests two independently engineered replacement targeting criteria against a pre-declared SESOI of 0.02.

Results. Map ranking and regret ranking agree in 0 of 18 rankable cells (K6/K6b); the clearest case is screening: an unscreened space-filling design (`lhs`) beats screened classical DoE on the map by +0.105 to +0.221 AUC in all 24 cells tested, while `doe` still wins point regret by +0.0312 (p=0.0028) — a complete ranking reversal from one structural difference. On Murphy decomposition (P7, 12,000 rows, 10 arms), SPADE's three variants take refinement ranks 1–3 of 9 (sharpest in the study) and calibration ranks 5–7 of 9 (below-average reliability); a plain scrambled Sobol' sequence is simultaneously best on calibration, Brier score and empirical containment, using one plate, no model, no adaptivity. DoE's classical fit is not merely worst on calibration — at 0.22959 it is 5.2× worse than the next-worst arm and 7.9× worse than Sobol'. The certificate itself, re-audited at a corrected 4,096-draw count after an earlier claimed failure was shown to be a 512-draw Monte Carlo artefact (F3), holds at the hill condition (0 of 18 cells below nominal at γ up to 0.99) but fails on Levy and Rosenbrock at γ=0.99 (3 of 64 cells, Holm-significant) and declines to certify almost anything on Ackley (0 of 24) or Hartmann6 (2 of 24), because the true acceptable region is measured to be near-empty on those families at the registered threshold grid — a mechanism predicted in advance from spread-design neighbour density, not discovered after the fact. The pre-registered prospective study (99,601 combined rows across seven conditions, `doe_unscreened` implemented at every d=6 condition) finds the targeted second plate does not earn its cost at the one registered TARGET cell: `spade_cf_m0` versus random plate-2 placement on symmetric-difference error, effect −0.00188, not significant (p_holm=0.41, KF-3 FAIL); versus a plate-1-only control, effect +0.01171 in the wrong direction (p_holm=1.3×10⁻⁴, KF-4 FAIL); spending part of Plate 2 on local exploitation does not lower regret either (KF-5 FAIL). SPADE remains within its pre-declared SESOI of Sobol' and qLogNEI on the map (KF-6/KF-7 PASS) and at practical regret parity with qLogNEI (KF-8 PASS), using 2 rounds against qLogNEI's 10. A follow-up study isolating two candidate repairs — an error-aware SUR criterion targeting expected map-error reduction directly, and a diversity-aware batch selector — against the same random-placement control and a 0.02 SESOI: both fail at the TARGET condition (effects +0.00146 and +0.00342, both worse than random, both non-significant); both show a real but sub-threshold improvement only on the harder cross-family cell, Hartmann6 (−0.00825, p_holm=0.0067; −0.01722, p_holm=2.2×10⁻⁶), roughly a third to two-thirds of the size needed to matter. The combined repair was never built because neither individual repair passed its own registered gate (KF-3d, MOOT).

Conclusions. Scoring a single recommended point, as the companion study's terminal-rule analysis shows, produces a benchmark result that is an artefact of which rule is used to read the point off the campaign. Scoring the design-space map instead produces a more stable ranking on the object closest to what an operating window is actually for, but it is not a free upgrade: it decomposes into sharpness (which SPADE wins) and reliability (which it does not), it is only validated on the one family and noise level (hill, σ up to 0.25, γ up to 0.99) where it has been prospectively and cross-family tested, and the specific mechanism this study built to make the second plate adaptive — boundary-targeted acquisition — does not demonstrate value over placing the same 8 wells at random, twice, after independent repair. What survives is the two-round *architecture* (a global space-filling round followed by one reactive round and a cross-fit certificate), not the specific *targeting rule* tested inside it. The study is not a wet-lab validation, and it does not claim SPADE beats any comparator in general.

1. Introduction

A laboratory that runs a matched-budget experimental campaign eventually has to hand someone a single deliverable: a recipe, or an operating window. The companion study (Yung, 2026) held everything about a Bayesian-optimization-versus-DoE comparison fixed except the rule used to produce that final deliverable, and found that the rule alone reverses which method looks better — DoE wins under a single noisy best-observed readout, BO wins by a wide margin under an unconstrained model-recommendation readout that turns out to be mostly an artefact of a saddle-shaped classical fit extrapolating off its own design region, and the two are statistically tied once the readout is constrained to the explored region or a laboratory-plausible three-well confirmation is added. That result is background for this paper, not its contribution; it is cited once, in full, in §2 below, and not re-derived.

The reversal raises a question the companion study does not answer: if the ranking is this sensitive to how a single point is read off a fixed set of wells, is a single point the right thing to be scoring? SPADE (Single-Plate Assured Design Envelope; ODIN-SPEC.md's successor, extended here to two plates) answers by changing the deliverable itself. Instead of a recipe, SPADE reports a probability-of-acceptability map over the whole design space and a certified operating region — the object a batch record or a regulatory submission (ICH Q8's "design space") actually wants. That reframing does not remove the measurement problem; it relocates it. A map can be sharp without being reliable, exactly as a point estimate can be precise without being accurate, and this paper's central methodological move is to insist on measuring both, rather than reporting one summary AUC that cannot see the difference (§3, §5.3).

The paper is organized around three questions, each with its own pre-existing failure this project had to correct before trusting the answer. Does the map agree with point regret about which method is best (§5.2)? Is the map, where it is sharp, also calibrated (§5.3)? And does the specific mechanism that makes SPADE's second plate adaptive — as opposed to just having a second plate — earn its complexity, tested prospectively rather than re-scored (§5.5–§5.6)? The third question is the one this project got wrong the most times before running it correctly: an earlier feasibility gate caught a registered threshold sitting above the certifiability ceiling before any campaign ran (§4.4); an earlier certificate "failure" was shown to be a 512-draw Monte Carlo artefact, not a property of SPADE (§5.4); and the eventual, correctly-gated answer is a negative result that survived two independent, principled repair attempts (§5.6).

1. Reader's guide

2.1 What the companion study found, and why it matters here

The companion study is not re-run or re-summarized in detail. Three numbers from it motivate this paper and are cited as background:

- Under the single-noisy-readout rule ("rule A," measured-value argmax), classical DoE beats BO at a matched budget: DoE − qLogEI = −0.0595 [−0.0792, −0.0373], p<0.0001 at the primary cell.
- Under the unconstrained model-recommendation rule, BO wins by +0.27 to +0.36 in every cell — a swing traced almost entirely to a classical quadratic fit being a saddle in 200 of 200 runs, forcing its unconstrained argmax onto a design-region boundary it was never fitted to describe.
- Constraining the classical recommendation to the explored region collapses that BO advantage to a null in 3 of 4 cells, and a three-well confirmation protocol collapses DoE's own rule-A advantage to a dead tie (−0.0009, null).

The throughline: which method "wins" is a property of the scoring rule at least as much as it is a property of the method. SPADE's response is not a fourth terminal rule for the same point-valued deliverable — it is a different deliverable.

2.2 Translating this paper's vocabulary

Map. A probability-of-acceptability surface, P(f(x) ≥ τ), computed from the posterior over the whole design box, plus a certified operating region (an inscribable hyperrectangle within which that probability provably clears a threshold at a stated confidence). Not a single recipe.

Symmetric-difference error. The primary map-quality scalar used here (not AUC, which was found to be invariant to a monotone transform and blind to a fitted surface with negative R² — see §5.2). Smaller is better; it is the volume where the fitted acceptable region and the true acceptable region disagree.

Calibration and refinement. A Brier score decomposes exactly into these two numbers (verified to residual 2.2×10⁻¹⁶ on every row tested). Refinement is sharpness/resolution — how confidently the map commits, one way or the other; higher is better. Calibration is reliability — when the map says "80% likely acceptable," is it right 80% of the time; lower is better. A map can be sharp and wrong, or vague and right. AUC alone cannot tell these apart.

Certificate. A conservative statistical guarantee that the certified region really is acceptable at a stated joint confidence γ and threshold fraction τ_frac, computed via cross-fit (split-draw) containment rather than checking the region against the same posterior draws that built it — the latter is circular by construction.

γ and τ_frac. γ is the assurance level demanded (e.g., 0.95, 0.99). τ_frac is the acceptability bar as a fraction of that family's own achievable maximum. A common τ_frac does not pose the same question on every landscape: measured true prevalence of the acceptable region at τ_frac=0.60 ranges from 0.0% (Ackley) to 95.6% (Rosenbrock) — the same nominal setting is nearly empty on one family and nearly the whole box on another.

SESOI. Smallest effect size of interest, 0.02 on the map-error scale, pre-declared before any prospective campaign ran. An effect that is statistically significant but smaller than the SESOI is reported as real but not material.

Plate / round / well. A well is one formulated recipe. A round (plate) is one formulate-incubate-read-decide cycle. SPADE spends 48 wells over 2 rounds; the companion study's named BO arm spends the same 48 wells over 10 rounds; screened classical DoE spends 3 rounds; a one-shot space-filling design spends 1.

KF ledger. Ten pre-registered kill conditions (KF-1 through KF-10), each with a firing rule fixed before any prospective campaign ran, adjudicating specific claims (does the certificate hold; does targeted Plate 2 beat random Plate 2; does local exploitation lower regret; and so on). PASS/FAIL/INCONCLUSIVE/MOOT/NOT_RUN are the only allowed verdicts, and each is defined in the registration, not chosen after seeing data.

Re-score versus prospective. A re-score re-reads already-committed wells under a new rule; it can test a scoring convention but cannot test an allocation rule, because the allocation already happened. A prospective run generates Plate 2's wells live, from the frozen rule applied to Plate 1's live fit. Only a prospective run licenses "a lab running this protocol would see X." Most of this project's earlier SPADE evidence (Version B, K6, P6–P8) is a re-score; §5.5–§5.6 report the first prospective test.

2.3 How to read the results tables

The same conventions as the companion study: Wilcoxon signed-rank p-values for paired campaigns (same landscape, both arms), bootstrap intervals for effect size, Holm–Bonferroni correction when several arms are compared against one reference in a shared family. A result reported PASS against a SESOI means the observed difference, even if statistically detectable, is smaller than the pre-declared threshold of practical interest — not that no difference exists.

1. Theoretical background

3.1 The map as the estimand

Let x ∈ [0,1]^d denote a formulation and f(x) its noiseless quality. Rather than nominating a single x*, SPADE reports, from a posterior fit to noisy observations y = f(x) + ε, the surface P(f(x) ≥ τ | data) and a certified region R such that P(min_{x∈R} f(x) ≥ τ) ≥ γ, evaluated conservatively. The map-quality object is the symmetric difference between the fitted acceptable region {x : P(f(x)≥τ) ≥ 0.5} and the true acceptable region {x : f(x) ≥ τ}, integrated over the box — a proper scoring object recommended over AUC once AUC was shown to be invariant to any monotone transform of the fitted probabilities and therefore blind to a fitted surface that is worse than a constant-mean baseline (grid_r² negative for all 8 arms tested including DoE, −6.19 for DoE specifically).

3.2 Calibration/refinement decomposition

The Brier score B = (1/n)Σ(p_i − o_i)² for a set of n predicted-probability/binary-outcome pairs decomposes exactly (Murphy, 1973) into calibration (grouped predicted-vs-observed reliability, lower better) and refinement (resolution, higher better), verified here to hold to floating-point residual on every row. A map can rank first on refinement — the sharpest posterior in the study — and simultaneously rank in the bottom half on calibration, meaning its confident answers are more often wrong than a less sharp competitor's. AUC, computed from the same probabilities, cannot see this: it is threshold- and monotone-transform-invariant, so a well-calibrated vague map and a poorly-calibrated sharp map can post identical AUC.

3.3 The certificate and its cross-fit correction

An excursion-set certificate built from posterior draws and checked against those same draws is circular: the region was chosen to look good on exactly the data used to grade it. Cross-fit (split-draw) containment splits the posterior draws into a selection half (used to build the region) and a held-out half (used only to grade it), removing that circularity. This project's own earlier same-draw estimate at 512 draws produced an apparent high-assurance certificate failure that a later, corrected re-run at 1,024+ draws showed was Monte Carlo noise in the containment estimate itself, not a property of the method (§5.4) — the same class of mistake the founding specification's own OA-LHS Stein-guarantee citation warns against dropping silently.

3.4 τ_frac is not a common question across families

Because τ is defined as a fraction of each family's own achievable maximum, a fixed τ_frac implies wildly different true prevalence of the acceptable region across families (0.0% on Ackley to 95.6% on Rosenbrock at τ_frac=0.60), and the certifiability ceiling itself, tau_max(γ, σ), falls as γ rises — so "γ=0.99" is not mechanically "the hardest corner"; at high σ it can correspond to a lower absolute bar and a larger true acceptable region than a lower γ. Any cross-family or cross-γ certificate claim must therefore be read per-cell, never pooled.

1. Methods

4.1 SPADE protocol as implemented (versus as specified)

Plate 1: n=40–48 wells, space-filling. Plate 2: 8 wells, placed either at random (`spade_random_plate2`, the causal control), by boundary SUR/straddle targeting alone (`spade_cf_m0`), or with 4 or 8 of those 8 wells redirected to local exploitation inside the currently-certified region (`m4`, `m8`). A conservative excursion certificate is computed from the joint posterior over both plates, cross-fit at N_DRAWS=4096 (split 2,048/2,048).

Two founding specification stages were never built, and every result in §5 inherits both gaps:

- Plate 1's specified design (a 49-point strength-2 orthogonal-array LHS, stratifying every 1D marginal and 2D projection) was never implemented; every SPADE arm's Plate 1 here is plain LHS, which only stratifies 1D marginals. A companion measurement found plain-LHS design variance alone accounts for over half of the entire gap between a one-shot spread GP and 10-round BO on a hard cross-family benchmark — the mechanism that would reduce that draw-to-draw lottery was never built or measured as a headline result here.
- The diagnosed fix for SPADE's calibration shortfall — a replicate-pooled noise estimate, plugged into the GP and held fixed rather than fitted jointly with the lengthscale — was never built as a working estimator, but its best possible case was bounded directly: K1 substitutes the oracle-true noise variance for the plugin estimate on all 200 stored campaigns and measures the ceiling improvement in Rule-P regret at +0.00816, 95% CI [+0.00202, +0.01417], p=0.0166 — statistically real, and below the registered 0.01 bar. No achievable finite-replicate estimator can beat an oracle it is bounded by, so this fix is not worth building regardless of estimator quality (Piece A, killed). Neither of the founding specification's two never-built pieces closes the gap it was hypothesized to close, once each was actually tested: Plate 1's specified strength-2 orthogonal-array design was also built and measured directly (K2) — design-draw standard deviation on Hartmann6 is 0.1628 (n=49, 25 seeds × 5 draws) against plain LHS's committed 0.1496 (Q53), a difference of +0.0132, 95% CI [−0.0196, +0.0450], spanning zero and trending the wrong direction (Piece B, killed). Both results are the last two commits in this project's SPADE work and post-date this paper's other consolidated findings documents, which had recorded these two pieces as merely unbuilt rather than as tested and killed.

4.2 Why re-scoring cannot test an allocation rule

A re-score reads already-committed wells under a new scoring rule; because the wells were already chosen, a re-score can change how a fixed campaign is graded but cannot test whether a different allocation rule would have chosen different, better wells. The question "does spending part of Plate 2 on local exploitation buy regret without costing the certificate" is answerable only prospectively, which is why the confirmatory study (§4.4) generates Plate 2 live from each campaign's own Plate-1 fit rather than replaying a stored design.

4.3 Certificate and map audits (K6/K6b, P6, P7, P8, F3)

K6/K6b compares map ranking (symmetric-difference error) against regret ranking across 8 campaign arms on the Hill oracle (9,600+1,600 rows). P6 repeats the cross-family map comparison at 96,000 rows across Hartmann6, Levy, Rosenbrock and Ackley at d∈{6,8}. P7 computes the Murphy calibration/refinement decomposition across 10 arms, 500 campaigns, 12,000 rows. P8 audits the certificate cross-family (5 families × 4 SPADE arms × 50 seeds, 24,000 rows) at the corrected 4,096-draw count, scored per (family, γ, τ_frac) cell, never pooled. F3 re-runs the original certificate cells at 512/1,024/2,048/4,096 draws (250 campaigns, 3,000 rows) to test whether an earlier apparent high-assurance failure was a draw-count artefact.

4.4 The prospective confirmatory study (`spade-final-2026-08-23`)

Registered at commit c4f58d3, before any prospective campaign ran. Freezes: three SPADE Plate-2 arms (m0/m4/m8) and local rule L1 (trust region = the ARD ball of radius 1, the same statistic governing the identification-versus-search gap in the companion study, not a tuned box); two causal controls (`spade_random_plate2`, `spade_plate1_only`); a mandatory 13-arm registry including qLogEI, qLogNEI, DoE (screened and unscreened), LHS, Sobol' and random; N_DRAWS=4096 with cross-fit split; four Holm families; and ten kill-ledger items (KF-1–KF-10) with firing conditions fixed in advance. Seven conditions were registered: one TARGET (hill, d=6, σ=0.10 — the only cell in which a kill verdict is adjudicated), and six ROBUSTNESS/EXCEPTION conditions (hill σ=0.25; Hartmann6 d=6 and d=8; Ackley, pre-declared EXCEPTION; Levy; Rosenbrock) providing context but no kill verdict. A pre-run feasibility gate (§5.1) screens every (condition, threshold) cell for certifiability before any campaign is spent on it.

An erratum (Erratum 1) was committed before any prospective result file existed — checkable from the commit graph — changing the threshold estimand to a per-family prevalence quantile (τ_q) and making the primary γ σ-dependent (0.95 at σ=0.10, 0.50 at σ=0.25, with γ=0.95 retained as a diagnostic at σ=0.25). This changes which cells are evaluable; it relaxes no decision rule, SESOI, Holm family, or kill condition.

4.5 The KF-3 follow-up (`spade-kf3-followup-2026-08-24`)

KF-3's FAIL left three candidate explanations conflated: a misaligned acquisition proxy, poor batch clustering, or a genuine budget/power limit (8 wells is not enough for any criterion to separate itself from random). Two replacement mechanisms were built test-first and isolated into independently registered arms against the same `spade_random_plate2` control, each tested against a pre-declared SESOI of 0.02: `spade_cf_erroraware` (a Bect et al.-style SUR criterion targeting expected symmetric-difference reduction directly, rather than the original straddle-score proxy) and `spade_cf_diverse_batch` (the original straddle criterion with a repulsion-penalized batch selector replacing hard exclusion). A combined arm (`spade_cf_erroraware_diverse`) was pre-registered as gated on both individual arms passing.

1. Results

5.1 The feasibility gate fired before any prospective campaign ran

A pre-run 20-campaign pilot at every registered (condition, threshold) cell found 12 of 14 cells INFEASIBLE — including the continuity condition, the one point in this project where SPADE's certificate had ever been measured — because the originally registered τ_f ∈ {0.60, 0.75} sat above the certifiability ceiling tau_max(γ=0.95, σ=0.25)=0.5888. This reproduces, in a different parameterization, a defect this project had registered a warning against and made again anyway; the gate caught it before compute was spent. The corrected estimand (τ_q, Erratum 1, §4.4) was adopted before any arm outcome existed.

5.2 Map ranking and regret ranking are different objects (K6/K6b, P6)

Map ranking agrees with regret ranking in 0 of 18 rankable cells. The cleanest case: an unscreened space-filling design (`lhs`) beats screened classical DoE (`doe`) on the map by +0.105 to +0.221 AUC, significant in all 24 (γ, τ_frac) cells tested, while `doe` still beats `lhs` on point regret (+0.0312, p=0.0028) — the two objects reverse from one structural difference (a 6→4 factor screen), holding everything else fixed. Screening's map cost generalizes off Hill and strengthens with dimension: at d=8, `doe` is worse on the map on 3 of 4 external families, and every one of 8 arms tested — including both BO arms — beats `doe` on the map (largest single effect: random vs. `doe`, +0.0517, p_holm=8×10⁻¹¹³). Screening's map cost runs one direction only: removing the screen entirely makes `doe`'s regret worse, not better (+0.206 to +0.207, p<1.4×10⁻⁴), so screening is a genuine adaptive-resource-allocation asset for point regret specifically, while costing the map — turning the screen off does not rescue the classical pipeline's map performance either (`doe_unscreened` still loses to every spread arm by more than the SESOI, p_holm<10⁻¹⁰).

On the standard test-function families (Hartmann6, d=6 and d=8, ROBUSTNESS conditions in the prospective study), the split reproduces in the opposite direction from what point regret alone would suggest: qLogNEI/qLogEI dominate point regret (1st/2nd, wide margin), but on symmetric-difference map error the three SPADE arms take 1st, 2nd and 3rd of 11 arms, ahead of Sobol' (4th) and far ahead of qLogNEI (9th) and qLogEI (worst, 11th) — at both d=6 and d=8. This is not adjudicated as a kill (Hartmann6 is a ROBUSTNESS, not TARGET, condition), but it is reported because it is the same split, in reverse, as the screening result above: which method wins depends entirely on which of the two objects — point or region — is being asked about.

This is reported as an architecture property. §4.1 diagnosed a specific fix for exactly this kind of GP under-smoothing — a replicate-pooled noise estimate — and it is now closed, not open: K1 shows that even an oracle-granted, perfect noise-variance ceiling (not merely an achievable estimator) improves Rule-P regret by only +0.00816 [+0.00202, +0.01417], below the registered 0.01 bar, so no realizable version of this fix could close the gap. The calibration shortfall is better read as a property of the architecture than of one unbuilt estimator. A separate model-internal statistic (alpha_star, a functional of the fitted posterior alone) was investigated as a possible calibration proxy and explicitly ruled out: it ranks DoE (5.2× worst on calibration) first and Sobol' (best on calibration, Brier and containment) last, because it measures willingness to assert an excursion, not correctness of the assertion.

5.4 The certificate: corrected, then bounded by family and γ (F3, P8)

An earlier registered claim — "SPADE's certificate fails below nominal containment at high assurance (γ≥0.95)" — is withdrawn. A draw-count sweep (250 campaigns, 3,000 rows) at the same cells that produced that claim found all originally sub-nominal cells reach or exceed nominal containment by 1,024 posterior draws (worst cell: 0.860 → 0.980) and stay there through 4,096, with no cell significantly below nominal at 4,096 draws even before Holm correction. The registered mechanism prediction — that the bias scaled with the number of scored candidates — was tested directly and failed (containment identical at n_rho=16 and n_rho=64 at every draw level); the bias was Monte Carlo noise in the containment estimator at low draw counts. A small residual selection bias (1.5–3.5 percentage points, concentrated at the highest-γ cells where Vorob'ev quantiles tie) survives at 4,096 draws and is corrected by cross-fitting.

Tested properly off Hill, at the already-corrected 4,096-draw count (1,000 campaigns, 5 families × 4 SPADE arms × 50 seeds, 24,000 rows, 0 gate failures), the certificate has a real, family-specific boundary:

| ---------- | ---- | ------ | --------- | ----- | --------- |
| levy       | 0.99 | 0.60   | 34/49     | 0.694 | 6.05×10⁻⁷ |
| rosenbrock | 0.99 | 0.60   | 37/50     | 0.740 | 4.74×10⁻⁵ |
| levy       | 0.99 | 0.75   | 37/48     | 0.771 | 1.22×10⁻³ |


All three failing cells are at γ=0.99; Hill has zero cells below nominal (18 of 18 scored cells clear 0.95, worst 0.980). Ackley and Hartmann6 do not fail — they decline to certify anything at all (0 of 24 and 2 of 24 cells produce a nonempty certified region), because the true acceptable region at the registered threshold grid is measured to be near-empty on those families (prevalence 0.00000 on Ackley, 0.00805 on Hartmann6 at τ_frac=0.60, versus 0.736–0.956 on Hill/Levy/Rosenbrock) — a mechanism predicted in advance from spread-design neighbour density, not discovered after the fact. No containment claim may be stated family-agnostically: "the certificate is valid on Hill at γ up to 0.99, and under-covers on Levy/Rosenbrock at γ=0.99 specifically" is the correct statement; "the certificate is valid" alone is not. An attempted automatic scope detector — meant to flag from data alone whether a new family/condition falls inside the certificate's validated scope — fired 0 of 50 correct on both held-out families and was not shipped; scope must be declared in advance, per family and γ, as done here, because no working automatic detector exists.

The "declines to certify almost anything on Ackley or Hartmann6" finding was tested a second way, using a per-family, quantile-scaled target region rather than a fixed τ_frac (400 campaigns, 9,600 rows, 0 gate failures): a looser, quantile-relative target does raise the non-empty rate on both families — Ackley from 0% to 13.3% pooled (41.9% at the loosest tested target, p=0.30) and Hartmann6 from 8.3% to 24.7% pooled (81.2% at p=0.30) — but the pre-registered bar of "at least half of cells non-empty" does not clear in aggregate for either family, even though Hartmann6 clears it decisively at the single loosest setting. This is a real, worthwhile scoring correction, not a null result, but it narrows rather than reverses the finding above: both families remain largely unable to certify small target regions at 48 wells, regardless of scoring convention.

Cross-cutting caveats on generalization. Every "targeting doesn't earn its cost" verdict here rests on Hill alone at the one TARGET cell — on one registered Ackley seed, all four two-plate variants post bit-identical regret while their map metrics differ, meaning regret has zero power there to separate targeted from random Plate 2 at all, and only the map can; whether that changes the Hill verdict cannot currently be determined from this evidence base. SPADE's own Plate-2 acquisition targets a fixed design threshold (θ = 0.75·mu_max) unmodified per family; measured coverage of that target against each family's actual grid is 0.0000 on Ackley and 0.0020 on Hartmann6 — SPADE's Plate 2 is not meaningfully targeting anything on those two families in the cross-family map table (§5.2), a real and unresolved design/family mismatch that should be weighed before reading those numbers as SPADE operating as intended.

5.8 Overall: SPADE is a low-variance middling arm, not a general winner

On the performance-profile convention (fraction of problems where an arm lands within τ× the best arm on that problem, computed at σ=0.10 under the posterior-mean terminal rule), SPADE wins outright on 2% of problems and lands within 3× of the best arm on 70% — rarely best, rarely catastrophic. Classical DoE matches the 2% outright-win rate but is within 3× of the best arm on only 22%, carrying a long, heavy tail that SPADE does not. This governs every claim in this paper: SPADE is not shown to be superior to Sobol', BO or DoE in general, on either object.

1. Discussion

Two findings recur across this paper's tables in a way that reframes the companion study's terminal-rule result rather than merely echoing it. First, map quality and point regret are consistently different objects — 0 of 18 rankable cells agree, and the direction of disagreement itself flips by family (screening helps regret and costs the map on Hill/cross-family; the same split runs in reverse on Hartmann6, where BO dominates regret and SPADE dominates the map). A benchmark that reports only one of the two objects is making the companion study's mistake at the population level rather than the point level: it is choosing, implicitly, which deliverable to score. Second, within the map itself, sharpness and reliability separate the same way point-estimate accuracy and confidence can separate for a single recipe — SPADE's refinement ranks are the best in the study and its calibration ranks are below average, and the one architecture that is uniformly strong on the validated reliability metrics (Sobol') is also the simplest one in the comparison set.

The prospective confirmatory study is this paper's methodologically load-bearing result, independent of either finding above: it is a negative result about a specific mechanism, arrived at honestly, after the specific repairs its own failure predicted in advance were tried and also failed. That two independently engineered, principled fixes each produce a real but sub-threshold effect concentrated on the harder cross-family condition, and essentially nothing at the condition the method was designed for, is more informative than either fix succeeding or failing alone — it points at a budget/power limit (eight wells in six dimensions) rather than a fixable defect in either criterion, and it is stated as such rather than smoothed into "the mechanism needs more engineering."

What survives, across every result in this paper, is not "SPADE's targeting rule works" — it demonstrably does not, at the registered budget — but "a second, reactive round plus a cross-fit certificate is a real and usefully cheap deliverable," at regret parity with a 10-round adaptive comparator and map parity (within SESOI) with the strongest simple space-filling baseline, in 2 rounds. The value in this evidence base is in the architecture existing at all, not in which rule chooses the second plate's wells.

1. Limitations and robustness checks

Two founding-specification stages were never built as working systems, and both were the leading candidates for closing this paper's gaps until each was directly tested and killed (§4.1, §5.3): the replicate-pooled noise estimator, bounded by an oracle-ceiling test at +0.00816 improvement against a registered 0.01 bar, and Plate 1's specified strength-2 orthogonal-array design, whose measured design-draw variance on Hartmann6 (0.1628) is statistically indistinguishable from plain LHS's (0.1496, diff 95% CI spanning zero and trending the wrong direction). A companion measurement elsewhere in this project found plain-LHS design variance alone accounts for over half of a comparable spread-design-vs-adaptive-BO gap; that mechanism does not transfer to an orthogonal-array fix once actually built and measured. Both pieces remain absent from the implementation graded in §5 — the ceiling test is not the estimator, and OA-LHS was measured but never substituted into a live SPADE Plate 1 — but neither can still be cited as an open, unmeasured possibility for moving this paper's headline numbers. Every "targeting doesn't earn its cost" verdict in §5.5–§5.6 rests on the single registered TARGET cell (Hill, d=6, σ=0.10); one Ackley seed shows regret has zero power to distinguish targeted from random Plate 2 at all on that family, and whether that generalizes to Hill is untested. The certificate's validated scope is narrow and family-specific (§5.4); no automatic detector exists to flag when a new condition falls outside it. Hill itself, the one family the certificate is validated on, is a synthetic construction whose structural assumptions (all six factors biphasic with an interior optimum; a 4.5–9× active-to-inert factor ratio) are contradicted by the one real dataset this project can compare against, and the noise level used throughout (σ=0.25) is optimistic relative to that same real anchor by roughly 2.7×. Dimension 8 is untested for the certificate audit (P8 is d=6 only). No wet-lab validation exists at any point in this paper.

1. Conclusions

Map quality and point regret are different objects. 0 of 18 rankable cells agree; scoring only one reproduces, at the population level, the same instability the companion study found at the single-recipe level.

SPADE's map is sharp, not reliable. Refinement ranks 1–3 of 9; calibration ranks 5–7 of 9. A plain scrambled Sobol' sequence is the strongest simple baseline on calibration, Brier score and containment.

The certificate is real and correctable, then bounded by family and γ. A 512-draw "failure" was an estimator artefact, not a method failure (F3). Valid on Hill through γ=0.99; under-covers on Levy/Rosenbrock at γ=0.99; largely unable to certify anything on Ackley or Hartmann6 because the acceptable region is measured near-empty there. A quantile-relative target region narrows this finding; it does not reverse it.

Neither never-built founding-specification piece is still an open possibility. A replicate-pooled noise estimator is bounded by an oracle-ceiling test below its own registered 0.01 bar (K1, killed). A strength-2 orthogonal-array Plate 1 measures no reduction in design-draw variance over plain LHS on the family tested (K2, killed).

Targeted Plate 2 does not earn its complexity. Boundary-targeted acquisition does not beat placing the same eight wells at random, prospectively tested (KF-3, KF-4 FAIL) and twice independently repaired (KF-3b, KF-3c FAIL) — both repairs show a real but sub-threshold effect confined to the hardest cross-family condition.

What is retained is the architecture, not the rule. A global space-filling round followed by one reactive round and a cross-fit certificate holds regret parity with a 10-round adaptive comparator (2 rounds vs. 10, KF-8 PASS) and map parity within SESOI of the strongest space-filling baseline (KF-6/KF-7 PASS). No claim of general superiority over Sobol', BO or DoE is made or supported.
